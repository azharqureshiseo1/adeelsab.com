"No hidden fees" is one of the most overused phrases in online selling. Sellers in Pakistan have heard it before, then found deductions in their payout that nobody mentioned.

So instead of a slogan, here is exactly what we mean, and how to check we're keeping our word.

## The promise

**If a fee is not listed on our [pricing page](/pricing), we don't charge it.**

That page lists every fee a seller can pay on AdeelSab: commission by category, listing, shipping, COD handling, RTO and payout transfer. Next to each one, it says when it applies.

## Why we put the uncomfortable costs first

The costs sellers most often discover too late are the ones we put front and centre:

- **RTO charges.** What you pay when a COD parcel is refused. They're on the [delivery page](/delivery#rto), with a table by zone.
- **Payout timing.** When cash actually reaches you. See the schedule on the [payouts page](/payouts).
- **Commission by category.** The full table is visible on the page, not behind a "contact us" button.

A seller who knows these numbers before signing can price correctly from day one. A seller who finds them in their first payout feels cheated, and they're right to.

## Why some numbers say "to be confirmed"

We're pre-launch. Some rates, such as specific commission percentages and delivery charges, are still being finalised with our partners. Rather than publish estimates that might change, we mark those values clearly until they're confirmed.

We'd rather show you an honest gap than a number we might have to walk back.

## How to hold us to it

- **Compare your payout statement with the pricing page.** Every deduction should match a listed fee.
- **Ask during onboarding.** Our team will go through every cost with you, in Urdu or English, before you sign.
- **Tell us if something doesn't match.** Message us on WhatsApp. If we've charged something that isn't listed, that's our mistake to fix.

## Why this matters to us

A marketplace only works if sellers trust it with their stock, their customers and their money. Trust built on a slogan breaks at the first surprise deduction. Trust built on published numbers lasts.
