A parcel travels through pickup, a sorting hub, a truck and a rider's bike before it reaches the customer. It gets stacked, dropped and sometimes rained on. Good packaging protects the product, your rating and your margin.

## Choose the right size

The packaging should fit the product closely.

- **Too big** and the product moves around and breaks.
- **Too small** and the corners burst open.

Aim for about 2–3 cm of space around the product for padding.

## Materials by product type

- **Clothing, textiles, soft goods.** Seal the item in a clear polythene bag first to keep it dry and clean, then place it in a courier flyer.
- **Shoes.** Keep them in the shoe box, then put the shoe box inside a flyer or outer carton. Never send a shoe box with a label stuck straight on it.
- **Cosmetics and liquids.** Tighten the cap, tape it shut, and seal the bottle in a zip bag. Wrap it in bubble wrap and pack it in a small carton, not a flyer.
- **Electronics and accessories.** Keep the original box. Wrap it in bubble wrap and pack it in a carton.
- **Glass, ceramics and fragile items.** Use the double-box method below.

## Fragile items: the double-box method

1. Wrap the item in at least two layers of bubble wrap and tape the wrap.
2. Place it in a small inner box, filling gaps with crumpled paper so nothing moves.
3. Put the inner box inside a larger outer carton, with at least 5 cm of padding on every side.
4. Shake the box gently. If you hear or feel movement, add more padding.
5. Mark the outer box **FRAGILE**. Padding does the real work, but the label helps with handling.

## Sealing

- Use wide packing tape, not thin cellophane tape.
- Seal cartons with the **H-method**: one strip along the middle seam, and one strip across each edge seam.
- Don't use rope or string. It catches on sorting machines.
- Reusing a box is fine if it is strong. Remove or cover all old labels and barcodes.

## Labels and paperwork

- Stick the shipping label flat on the largest surface, not over a seam or edge.
- Cover the label with clear tape if the package might get wet, but not over the barcode's scan area if it causes glare.
- Put an invoice or packing slip inside the parcel listing the items. It helps the customer check the order, and helps us resolve disputes.

A compact parcel still matters, because it travels more safely:

- Use flyers instead of cartons for soft items.
- Don't use a box much bigger than the product.
- Fold clothing neatly instead of rolling it loosely.

## Record a packing video

For higher-value orders, record a short, uncut video on your phone while packing. Show the product, any serial number, the sealing and the label. If a customer later reports a missing or damaged item, this video is your strongest evidence. See [returns and disputes](/seller-hub/returns-and-disputes).

## Packaging checklist

- Product checked for defects before packing
- Right size packaging, with 2–3 cm of padding
- Liquids sealed, fragile items double-boxed
- H-method taping with wide tape
- Old labels removed, new label flat and visible
- Invoice or packing slip inside
- Packing video for high-value orders
