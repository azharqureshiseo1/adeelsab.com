Customers can't touch your product. Your photos are the closest they get. You don't need a camera or a studio. A phone from the last few years, a window and a white sheet are enough.

## What you need

- Your phone, with the lens wiped clean. A fingerprint on the lens is the most common cause of blurry photos.
- A window with daylight
- A plain white or light-grey background: a bedsheet, chart paper or a white wall
- Something to lean the product against, such as a box or a stack of books
- Ten minutes per product

## Light: use the window, not the flash

Daylight from a window is the best free light there is.

1. Set up next to a window, with the light coming from the side.
2. Avoid direct harsh sunlight. It creates hard shadows. Late morning or afternoon light through a curtain is ideal.
3. Turn off the flash and the room's tube lights. Mixed light makes white look yellow or blue.
4. If one side is too dark, hold a white paper or thermocol sheet on that side to bounce light back.

## Background: plain and clean

- Drape a white sheet so it curves from the wall down onto the table. This hides the corner and gives a clean, seamless look.
- Iron out the creases, or pull the sheet tight.
- Remove everything else from the frame: other products, bags, your hand, the TV.

For clothing, a plain wall or a hanger against a plain door works well. Photograph suits laid flat on a clean bedsheet from directly above.

## Angles: show every side

Take at least four to six photos for each product:

1. **Front.** The main photo, product centred and filling most of the frame.
2. **Back and sides.**
3. **Close-up.** Fabric texture, stitching, buttons, the logo or the finish.
4. **Label or tag.** Size label, material tag, model number.
5. **Scale.** The product held in a hand or next to a common object, so size is obvious.
6. **In use.** Worn, or placed on a table, if you can.

## Phone settings that help

- **Don't zoom.** Digital zoom loses detail. Move the phone closer instead.
- **Tap to focus** on the product before taking the photo.
- **Turn on the grid** in your camera settings to keep the product straight and centred.
- **Hold still.** Rest your elbows on the table, or lean the phone against something.
- **Use the normal photo mode.** Portrait or beauty modes blur edges and change colours.

## Be honest in every photo

Photos that make a product look better than it is lead directly to refused parcels and returns.

- **No filters.** Colour must match the real product.
- **Show defects.** If there's a mark or a small flaw, photograph it and mention it in the description.
- **Use your own photos.** Don't copy photos from other sellers or the internet. If the customer receives something that looks different, they will refuse it, and copied photos can break copyright rules.

## Quick edits (only if needed)

Your phone's built-in editor is enough:

- **Crop** to a square, keeping the product centred.
- **Straighten** if the phone was tilted.
- **Brightness** up slightly if the photo looks dull.

Don't change the colour or saturation, and don't remove real flaws.

## Checklist before sending photos

- Lens cleaned
- Daylight from the side, flash off
- Plain background, nothing else in frame
- Front, back, close-up, label and scale photos
- Sharp focus, no zoom
- Colours match the real product
